Victorian 2.0 vsAustralian Curriculum 9.0

A complete, code-by-code comparison of the HPE content descriptions for Levels 7–8 and 9–10. Built for teachers adapting Victorian resources for interstate use, and for Victorian faculties checking that their documentation reflects what Victoria actually requires.

The single most common mapping error

Victoria has ten movement content descriptions per band. The Australian Curriculum has nine. The extra Victorian description sits at position four — VC2HP8M04 and VC2HP10M04, covering movement composition — and has no Australian equivalent.

Because it is inserted rather than appended, every Victorian movement code from M05 onward sits one position ahead of its Australian counterpart. VC2HP8M06 maps to AC9HP8M05, not AC9HP8M06. A straight number-for-number substitution will be wrong for six of ten movement codes in each band.

What Victoria requires that the Australian Curriculum does not

1. First aid and CPR are mandated content

VC2HP8P08 adds “(including basic first aid and CPR)”. VC2HP10P08 adds “(including first aid and CPR)”. The Australian Curriculum descriptions contain no such wording. A Victorian HPE program with no first aid or CPR component is not meeting these descriptions at either band.

2. Online consent is explicit

VC2HP8P07 requires consent skills “across multiple settings (including online and offline)”. The Australian Curriculum wording stops at consent generally. Digital consent is named Victorian content, not an extension activity.

3. Aquatic settings are named

VC2HP8M01 and VC2HP10M01 specify “indoor, outdoor and aquatic settings”. AC9HP8M01 and AC9HP10M01 do not mention aquatic settings at all. This is the curriculum-level basis for Victoria’s mandated swimming and water safety requirement, and four of Victoria’s twelve focus areas refer specifically to swimming and water safety.

4. Movement composition is assessable

VC2HP8M04 and VC2HP10M04 require students to adapt, perform and evaluate movement sequences using the movement elements. Gymnastics and dance are the two focus areas that carry this. A program with no composition component leaves two of twenty content descriptions unaddressed per band.

5. Community scope is wider at Levels 9–10

Several Victorian health descriptions extend the Australian framing from “their own and others’” to “their own, others’ and community’s” — see VC2HP10P09 and VC2HP10P10. That extension is what justifies a community-facing assessment task rather than a personal-reflection one.

Using this in the other direction

If you are outside Victoria and adapting Victorian resources, the health strand transfers almost intact — the P codes align one-for-one by number and most differ only in verb or scope. The movement strand needs the offset applied, and the first aid, CPR and aquatic components are additions you may keep but are not required to teach.

If you are in Victoria and your documentation was built from Australian Curriculum materials, check the movement codes first. That is where the error will be.

Sources and how to check this yourself

Victorian wording is taken verbatim from the VCAA Version 2.0 curriculum export for Health and Physical Education. The VCAA Version 2.0 site is a JavaScript application and its content descriptions are not reliably indexed by search engines — the Download curriculum function is the authoritative way to obtain them.

Australian Curriculum wording is taken from the published Years 7–10 sequence of content descriptions. Australian Curriculum material is © ACARA 2010–present, licensed CC BY 4.0.

Both curricula are revised periodically. Verify against the source before submitting curriculum documentation.
