Copyright, Ownership& Licensing

What you may do with these resources, who owns what, and where the licence stops. Written for teachers, faculty leaders and anyone republishing or adapting the suite.

The short version

You can use this. Freely. Copy it, print it, edit it, put it in your own unit plan, translate it, hand it to a colleague in another state, use it in paid professional learning. You need to do one thing: say where it came from.

What you cannot do is re-license the material that belongs to other organisations. That distinction is the whole point of this document.

Licence history — why this changed

The repository previously carried a GNU General Public License v3. GPL v3 is a software copyleft licence. Its core mechanics — source code distribution, object code, the definition of “the Program” — have no sensible application to lesson plans and PDFs, and its copyleft provisions would have required anyone incorporating a single worksheet into their own unit to release that whole unit under the GPL. Almost no teacher would do that, so in practice the old licence discouraged exactly the reuse it appeared to invite.

CC BY 4.0 was chosen over the NonCommercial and ShareAlike variants deliberately. NC would block Life Saving Victoria, AUSTSWIM or a swim school from using this material in paid professional learning — a legitimate and desirable distribution channel. SA would reintroduce the GPL problem. CC BY also matches ACARA, which licenses Australian Curriculum material under CC BY 4.0, so curriculum content and this suite can be blended without a licence conflict.

CC licences are irrevocable. Material released under CC BY 4.0 stays available under those terms for anyone who received it, even if the licence on this site changes later.

3. What the licence does not cover

Third-party material is excluded

This suite cites, quotes, links and builds on material owned by other organisations. None of it is sublicensed under CC BY 4.0. A licence can only be granted over what the licensor owns.

If you republish an adapted version of this suite, you must carry this limitation forward. Applying a blanket CC BY notice to a package containing third-party content misrepresents what you are entitled to grant.

4. Educational use of third-party material

Two provisions of the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth) are relevant to how this suite uses other organisations' material, and to how you use it in class.

Fair dealing for research or study

Sections 40 and 103C permit dealing with copyright material for research or study, subject to a fairness test that considers the purpose, the nature of the work, the amount taken and the effect on the market for the original. This supports quoting statistics and citing guidelines. It does not support reproducing a substantial part of someone else's resource and distributing it as your own.

The statutory educational licence

Part VB (now sections 113P and following) allows educational institutions to copy and communicate material for educational purposes under a statutory licence administered by the Copyright Agency. Most Australian schools are covered. This is what lets a teacher photocopy a chapter for a class — it is not a general permission to publish material on a public website or in a commercial product.

This document is general information, not legal advice. For anything involving commercial publication, translation for distribution, or reproduction of another organisation's material at scale, get advice from a copyright lawyer or the Copyright Agency.

Attribution required inside the files, not just on the site

Life Saving Victoria has confirmed it makes no intellectual property claim over this suite. Its only requirement is that LSV data carries a copyright attribution within the files that use it — not merely on the website or in this document.

Every resource in the suite that quotes drowning statistics now carries the formal citation in its own footer, and that citation is reproduced in the PDF and PowerPoint versions so it travels with a downloaded or emailed file. If you adapt any of these resources, retain those citations. Stripping them breaks the condition on which the data is used.

5. Data and statistics

Facts and statistics are not themselves protected by copyright — the expression of them is. You may state that Victoria recorded 52 fatal drownings in 2024–25. You may not reproduce Life Saving Victoria's infographics, charts or report layout.

Statistics throughout this suite are current to:

  • Life Saving Victoria, Victorian Drowning Report 2024–25 (2025)
  • Royal Life Saving Australia, National Drowning Report 2026
  • Surf Life Saving Australia, Summer Coastal Drowning Report 2025/26 (2026)

Always cite the report and year. Drowning figures change annually and an uncited number rapidly becomes an unverifiable one.

6. AI-assisted development

This suite was developed with AI assistance for research, drafting, formatting and code generation. Educational judgement, curriculum alignment decisions, pedagogical sequencing and final content review are the author's.

The position on copyright in AI-assisted work is unsettled in Australia; the prevailing view is that material requires human authorship to attract copyright protection. The substantive intellectual contribution here — unit structure, curriculum mapping, task design, differentiation decisions — is human. This is disclosed rather than concealed, because teachers evaluating a resource are entitled to know how it was made.

7. If you find an error or a copyright problem

If you believe material in this suite infringes your copyright, or you spot a factual error, contact swimwater.safety@gmail.com. Errors are corrected and republished; anything genuinely infringing is removed.

Every attempt has been made to identify and correctly attribute third-party material. Where that has not been possible, corrections are welcomed.
